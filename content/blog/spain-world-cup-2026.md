I have supported Spain for as long as I have watched football. On 19 July 2026 they beat Argentina 1–0 after extra time in New Jersey and won their second World Cup, the first since 2010. In Bangladesh the final ended deep into the night. I did not sleep much, and I did not mind.

## A final with almost no chances

It was not a pretty game. Both teams were careful, and for long stretches nothing happened in either box. As a goalkeeper I watched the keepers more than anyone. Emiliano Martínez kept Argentina alive with save after save, and for a while it looked like he would take it to penalties on his own.

Late in normal time Argentina went down to ten men. Then, in the 106th minute, Ferran Torres came off the bench and scored. I shouted loud enough to wake the house.

## The goalkeeper's tournament

The part I loved most was quieter. Spain conceded only one goal in the whole tournament, and Unai Simón won the Golden Glove. People remember the goal, but a team that lets in one goal in eight games is built from the back. That is the kind of football I try to play.

## What I took from it

Spain did not panic when the goal would not come. They kept the ball, kept their shape and waited for their moment. I think that is a good way to study, too: do the boring parts properly and trust that the result will come.

Now I have a photo of a little World Cup trophy next to my laptop, and I am already counting the days to the next one.
