In goal, everything happens in a few seconds: a dive, a jump, a sprint off the line. A marathon is the opposite. In 2025 I ran one with Navana Runners and finished it, and I have the medal and the photo to prove it.

## Starting slow

The hardest part was holding back at the start. Everyone around me was excited and fast, and it felt wrong to let people pass. But the runners who had done this before all said the same thing: the race starts in the second half.

## The middle

There is a stretch in the middle where nothing is new. Your legs hurt a little, the finish is far away and there is nobody cheering. I broke it into small pieces: the next water point, the next turn, the next kilometre. It is the same trick I use for a long assignment.

## The finish

The last part was the best feeling I have had in sport outside a football match. I did not run fast, but I did not stop.

## What it taught me

Football taught me to react. The marathon taught me to keep going when there is nothing to react to. I want both in my studies: quick when the problem is in front of me, and patient for the parts that take months.
